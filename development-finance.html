The senior-debt picture, as of Q2 2026

Senior development debt for West Yorkshire residential schemes is running broadly in line with the rest of regional England — pricing starts in the high-6s to low-7s annualised for a well-presented £5m–£10m scheme to a 60% LTGDV with an experienced sponsor, and widens from there for higher gearing, weaker comparables, or lower-confidence exit assumptions.

What's specific to West Yorkshire in 2026 is which sub-markets attract competitive lender interest. Leeds central postcodes (LS1, LS2, LS9, LS10, LS11) get full-market competition; Bradford City Village area schemes get a smaller but improving lender shortlist; Wakefield and Kirklees get conventional regional lender appetite; Calderdale heavy-refurb projects get a much narrower list of specialists.

Mezzanine and stretched senior

Mezzanine activity in West Yorkshire 2026 is concentrated at the £5m–£20m scheme size where senior LTGDV is capped at ~60% and the sponsor wants to gear to 80–85%. A typical Leeds or Bradford SME deal stack looks like:

  • Senior debt — 60% of GDV at 6.9–8.0%, drawn in tranches against monitoring surveyor signoffs.
  • Mezzanine — a further 15–25% of GDV at 12–18% blended (cash coupon + PIK + arrangement), second-charge.
  • Sponsor equity — the residual 15–25% of GDV in cash, plus the land basis and any agreed development fee deferral.

The mezzanine market for West Yorkshire SME schemes is reasonably liquid in 2026 — there are 8–15 active mezz funders depending on ticket size, with pricing relatively stable year-on-year. The pinch point is process speed: from credit-paper to drawdown is typically 8–14 weeks for a clean deal, longer for anything with planning conditions still to discharge.

What's harder to fund in West Yorkshire right now

  • Pure-flat schemes outside Leeds / Bradford City Village. Lender appetite for stand-alone block-of-flats schemes in the smaller West Yorkshire markets is thinner than for mixed-house development.
  • Speculative student / co-living outside the LS1–LS9 belt. Without proximity to a major university campus and a clear operator strategy, these are difficult conversations.
  • Heavy-refurb conversions at high LTV. The capital exists, but pricing reflects the higher build-cost uncertainty — expect to gear lower or pay more.

What's easier to fund than you'd guess

  • 15–60 unit traditional housing schemes in Wakefield and Kirklees — competitive lender shortlists, normal pricing.
  • Bradford City Village adjacent residential — appetite has visibly improved in the last 12 months.
  • Stalled-site exit refinances — development exit finance is a deep market; multiple lenders compete on these in West Yorkshire.
